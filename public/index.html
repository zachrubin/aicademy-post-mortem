Timeline

From question bank to study platform

  1. Apr 2023

    The first pitch: a better question bank

    Our first deck, A New Approach to Studying, went after traditional question banks for being expensive, static, impersonal and barebones. The plan was MCAT-style passages and questions written by the GPT-4 API, with explanations you could ask follow-up questions about.

  2. May 2023

    aicademy Inc. is incorporated

    We formed the company, put up aicademy.one, and ran our first ads: Any problem. Any question. Get the answer with AI.

  3. May 23, 2023

    studio writes its first full document

    Given a template, a prompt and one of the student's own writing samples, studio drafted a complete medical school personal statement in the student's voice.

  4. Jun 2023

    In market, with paying users

    study, lab, studio and flash were live. We pitched aicademy as a comprehensive ecosystem of AI-powered study tools for students, recorded a pitch video, and reached 30 trial subscribers and 10 paying subscribers without spending anything on acquisition.

  5. Spring 2024

    Rebuilt for sharing

    We redesigned the data model around decks students could publish and share: follows, views, likes, referral-tracked share links, and a review schedule for every user and every card.

  6. 2024

    One unified study platform

    A refreshed pitch with a bigger goal: Every K-12 and higher ed student will use aicademy as a comprehensive platform to augment their education with AI.

  7. 2026

    Winding down

    With general-purpose assistants covering more of what we offered and less time to give it, we stopped development and closed out the company.

What we built

Four tools, one place to study

Each tool did one job, and they handed work to each other. Ask a question in study, turn the explanation into practice problems in lab, and send what you missed to flash.

Shipped · Ask

study

Ask any question and get a scholarly explanation at the level of detail you pick. Keep asking follow-ups, or turn the answer into questions and flashcards.

Shipped · Practice

lab

Unlimited adaptive practice problems on any topic, including passage-based sets with four answer choices and an explanation for each.

Shipped · Write

studio

AI writing tools built around the student's own words. Upload writing samples and studio drafts new work in that style or improves what's already there.

Shipped · Remember

flash

Flashcards generated from your weak spots, reviewed on a spaced-repetition schedule tuned for long-term retention.

Under the hood

What we built underneath

  • Generation on the GPT-4 API for explanations, practice questions, flashcards and writing.
  • Per-user spaced repetition. Each user had their own SM-2 state for every card: ease factor, interval, repetition count and next review date.
  • Passage-based question sets in the style of the MCAT, with ordered passages, four choices, a keyed answer and an explanation.
  • Social graph and sharing: public or private decks, follows, views, likes, and share links that tracked referrals.
  • Accounts with referral codes, subscription state and a dark mode, plus an in-app channel for feedback and bug reports.

Why we stopped

Two things happened at once

General-purpose assistants like ChatGPT got very good, very quickly. They became the default place students went to ask a question and get an explanation, which was the core of what study did. Features that set us apart in mid-2023 were showing up for free in tools students already had open.

At the same time, school and other commitments took more of our time each semester. Keeping ahead of the big models would have taken a full-time team, and we weren't in a position to be one. We chose to stop cleanly instead of letting the product decay.

Lessons

What we'd tell ourselves in 2023

  1. The model was never the moat

    We built on the GPT-4 API, and so could anyone. Whatever lasts lives around the model: review schedules, a student's history, class materials, and habits. We should have put more of our effort there, sooner.

  2. Nine products is too many for two people

    Our roadmap listed study, lab, studio, flash, sync, pathways, wellness, spaces and an institutional edition. Each made sense on its own. Together they spread a part-time team thin before any single tool was clearly the best option for one kind of student. Our first deck was only about MCAT prep, and that focus was worth keeping.

  3. Ten paying users is a signal, not a business

    Students paid $9.99 a month with no marketing, which told us the problem was real. It didn't tell us how to reach the next thousand. Distribution needed as much design as the product did.
